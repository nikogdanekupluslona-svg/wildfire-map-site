What does a Nebraska fire map show right now?

A Nebraska fire map layers official incident records, mapped perimeters, satellite thermal detections and National Weather Service fire-weather alerts. In grassland country, those layers can disagree sharply, because a wind-driven fire can add tens of thousands of acres between two official reports.

Incident records carry the confirmed figures: fire name, acres, percent contained, incident type and managing organization. On a rural Nebraska grass fire, a report filed at midday can already be far behind reality by evening if wind holds.

Satellite detections close that gap. NASA's MODIS and VIIRS instruments and NOAA's GOES satellites report heat within minutes to a few hours. On the live map, each detection carries a probability tier based on whether an official record is linked and how recent the perimeter and heat signals are.

Confirm before you actA thermal detection is a heat measurement, not a confirmed wildfire. It can also reflect a permitted agricultural burn. A hotspot with no official record attached is a question for your county emergency manager, not an answer.

When is Nebraska's wildfire season?

Most Nebraska wildfires occur between February and April, when the prior year's cured grass has not yet greened up and strong spring wind events cross the state. A second, less severe risk window follows summer curing into fall.

That pattern held through most of Nebraska's history, but 2026 broke it. A record-dry winter left fine fuels critically cured heading into March, and a wave of wind-driven fires that started March 12, 2026 burned into early July, an unusually long season by any prior Nebraska standard.

An outbreak that reset the record bookWinter 2025-26 was one of the warmest and driest on record in Nebraska. On March 13, 2026, Governor Jim Pillen declared a state of emergency as multiple fires burned out of control. "These fires present a significant and active danger, given current weather conditions," Pillen said, urging residents near fires to heed local authorities. The Nebraska Emergency Management Agency logged 24 wildfire reports in 24 hours. Source: High Plains Journal.

Which agencies fight fires in Nebraska?

The Nebraska Forest Service, a unit of the University of Nebraska-Lincoln, runs the state's wildland fire program: community wildfire protection plans, fuels reduction, fire danger forecasting through its Nebraska Wildfire Risk Assessment Portal, and emergency assistance for wildfire control. Initial attack, as in most plains states, is carried out by local and volunteer fire departments.

The Nebraska Emergency Management Agency, part of the Nebraska Military Department, activates the State Emergency Operations Center in Lincoln when a fire outgrows local capacity, coordinating National Guard aircraft, road graders from the Department of Transportation and mutual-aid crews from across the state.

During the March 2026 outbreak, that coordination put 26 Nebraska National Guard soldiers and airmen, fixed-wing fire suppression aircraft, an Army National Guard helicopter for aerial water drops, and Army and Air National Guard hand crews into the field within a day of the governor's emergency declaration, alongside a Complex Incident Management Team.

Nebraska sits within the Rocky Mountain Area Coordination Center's geographic area, alongside Colorado, Wyoming, Kansas and South Dakota. When Nebraska's fires exceed state resources, Rocky Mountain Area teams, including Complex Incident Management Team 1, are the ones dispatched to take command.

Where do Nebraska wildfires usually burn?

Nebraska's largest fires concentrate in the rolling grass-stabilized dunes of the Sandhills and the ranchland of the western Panhandle, both dominated by continuous native grass rather than cropland. The 2026 outbreak's worst damage fell across exactly this belt: Morrill, Garden, Arthur, Keith and Grant counties, all part of that open rangeland.

Central Nebraska's Platte and Republican river valleys, where the Road 702 and Cottonwood fires burned, mix cropland with riverside grass and brush, giving fire a corridor to run along even where farmland breaks up the surrounding terrain. Eastern Nebraska's denser cropland tends to produce smaller, more containable fires.

Nebraska's few forested pockets, most notably the Pine Ridge area near Chadron and Crawford in the northwest and the planted Nebraska National Forest near Halsey, add a ponderosa pine element largely absent from the rest of the state, though even fires near Halsey, including the 2026 Road 203 Fire, moved through the surrounding grassland as much as timber.

What happened in the 2022 Road 702 Fire?

The Road 702 Fire ignited in Kansas on April 22, 2022, and burned roughly 28 miles north along the Republican River corridor into Nebraska's Furnas and Red Willow counties, reaching about 68 square miles, roughly 43,500 acres, before crews reached 88 percent containment on April 28.

Wind gusts of 60 to 70 mph drove the fire that evening, with an 82 mph gust reported near Trenton. Retired Cambridge Fire Chief John P. Trumble, 66, was killed when his vehicle left the road amid zero-visibility smoke and dust while he was spotting for firefighting operations, a reminder that reduced visibility, not flame contact, is often the greater danger during a wind-driven grass fire.

Cambridge, Bartley, Indianola and Wilsonville were evacuated that evening, and Cambridge residents faced a second consecutive day of mandatory evacuation on April 23 as the fire continued to grow. U.S. Highway 6/34 closed between Arapahoe and McCook.

Recovery took months, not daysGovernor Pete Ricketts visited Cambridge afterward "to thank the fire chiefs, who worked really around-the-clock to be able to help bring that fire under control, and to survey the damage," adding that the state was "working on the recovery aspect of it." U.S. Rep. Adrian Smith called the damage "devastating," pledging to "do all I can to bring some relief" at the federal level. Source: Nebraska.tv.

What are the largest wildfires in Nebraska history?

Nebraska's wildfire record was rewritten in March 2026. The Morrill Fire started March 12 near an electrical pole, according to Governor Jim Pillen, who described it as "electrical in nature with wind popping wires and sparks." It burned 643,361 acres across Morrill, Garden, Arthur, Keith and Grant counties, traveling more than 70 miles in under six hours before crews reached 100 percent containment on March 25.

The Morrill Fire killed Rose White, 86, of Arthur County, when flames overtook her house, and forced evacuations around Lewellen and Lake McConaughy. Governor Pillen reflected on the scale of the season at a press briefing: "I've been on the Nebraska ground for 70 years, and what we just saw today is heart-wrenching, riveting to see mother nature attack the extraordinary people of Nebraska and the extraordinary land of Nebraska."

U.S. Sen. Pete Ricketts, a former governor, called it "probably... one of the largest fires, if not the largest fire, we've ever had in the state."

Together, the March through July 2026 outbreak burned roughly 930,000 acres statewide, prompting a federal major disaster declaration covering Arthur, Garden, Grant, Lincoln and Morrill counties. "There's risk across the state. The whole state's dry," Pillen said as later fires, including the South Fork Fire near Chadron, kept crews stretched into summer.

What weather drives Nebraska fire danger?

Nebraska fire danger, like the rest of the Central Plains, runs on the Grassland Fire Danger Index, which weighs wind, wind gusts, relative humidity and temperature but treats how cured the grass is as the dominant factor. Fully cured, brown grass carries fire far faster than green grass regardless of wind speed.

NWS Omaha/Valley, which covers eastern Nebraska, issues a Red Flag Warning when sustained winds reach 20 mph or gusts reach 30 mph for two or more hours alongside critically dry fuels, when relative humidity drops to 20 percent or below for two or more hours, when dry lightning covers more than 15 percent of critical fuels, or when grass curing exceeds 80 percent during near-record heat.

NWS North Platte covers western and central Nebraska with its own Fire Weather Planning Forecast and RAWS weather stations at Bessey, Crescent Lake, McKelvie and Valentine, feeding the same Rocky Mountain Area fire weather operating plan used during the 2026 outbreak.

How do evacuations work in Nebraska?

Nebraska county emergency managers issue evacuation orders using plain "mandatory evacuation" and "pre-evacuation" language rather than a single statewide numbered system, so read each county's own alert wording rather than assuming a term means the same thing everywhere. Cambridge, Nebraska used exactly this mandatory-evacuation language twice in three days during the Road 702 Fire in 2022.

During the 2026 outbreak, Lewellen and the area around Lake McConaughy were evacuated as the Morrill Fire approached, while residents of Oshkosh were told to activate lawn sprinklers as a defensive measure even before an evacuation order was issued for the town itself.

Register before fire seasonSign up for your county's alert system before spring. Nebraska's rural distances mean an evacuation order can arrive with very little lead time once a grass fire is running with the wind.

How do you track a Nebraska fire in under a minute?

Nebraska rewards speed of reading more than most states; the Morrill Fire covered more ground in six hours than many fires cover in a week.

  1. Open the live fire map and filter to Nebraska, or enable location to see fires within a set distance in miles.
  2. Check whether the detection has an official record attached, and read acres, containment, incident type and managing organization.
  3. Compare the "Last NIFC update," "Last satellite heat" and "Last GOES heat" timestamps; a wide gap matters most in grass fuels.
  4. Check for Red Flag Warnings or a Grassland Fire Danger outlook across your county and the county upwind.
  5. Note the wind direction; a Nebraska grass fire moves with the wind far more predictably than a timber fire.
  6. Confirm evacuation status for your own address directly with your county's alert system, not from the map alone.

Wind events that drive Nebraska fires often cross state lines, so compare with the Colorado and Wyoming maps, and see our guide to tracking current wildfires for the national picture.

Nebraska is the state where we most often see the gap between an agency update and reality open widest. The Morrill Fire moved more than seventy miles in under six hours, faster than most people would have believed possible, which is exactly why we timestamp every figure on US Wildfire Tracker separately: the last agency update, the last satellite heat detection, and the last GOES signal. When those three disagree sharply in a grassland state, trust the freshest one.

What does wildfire smoke do to Nebraska air quality?

Nebraska wildfire smoke is reported through the same EPA Air Quality Index used nationwide, where a value of 100 corresponds roughly to the short-term national air quality standard for a given pollutant. The March 2026 outbreak pushed hazardous air quality across Nebraska and into northern Colorado and northwestern Kansas as smoke plumes from multiple large fires combined downwind.

Frequently Asked Questions About the Nebraska Fire Map

What is the largest wildfire in Nebraska history?

The Morrill Fire, which started March 12, 2026, likely from sparks on an electrical pole per Governor Jim Pillen, and burned 643,361 acres across Morrill, Garden, Arthur, Keith and Grant counties before full containment on March 25. It killed one person, 86-year-old Rose White, and surpassed every previously recorded Nebraska wildfire.

What happened in the 2022 Road 702 Fire?

The Road 702 Fire started in Kansas on April 22, 2022 and burned north into Nebraska's Furnas and Red Willow counties, reaching roughly 43,500 acres. Retired Cambridge Fire Chief John P. Trumble was killed when his vehicle left the road in zero-visibility smoke, and Cambridge, Bartley, Indianola and Wilsonville were evacuated.

When does Nebraska's wildfire season peak?

Most Nebraska wildfires occur between February and April, before spring green-up, when cured grass and strong wind events combine. The 2026 season broke that pattern, with major fires burning from mid-March through early July after one of the driest winters on record.

Who fights wildfires in Nebraska?

Local and volunteer fire departments handle initial attack. The Nebraska Forest Service supports them with fire danger forecasting and fuels reduction, and the Nebraska Emergency Management Agency coordinates state resources, including National Guard aircraft, once a fire outgrows local capacity.

What do Nebraska evacuation orders mean?

Nebraska counties generally use "mandatory evacuation" and "pre-evacuation" language rather than a single statewide numbered system. Cambridge used mandatory-evacuation wording on consecutive days during the 2022 Road 702 Fire, so always read your county's specific alert text.

How current is the data on a Nebraska fire map?

Satellite heat detections from VIIRS, MODIS and GOES arrive within minutes to a few hours of a pass. Official incident records update whenever a responding agency files a report, which can lag well behind a wind-driven grass fire. US Wildfire Tracker refreshes automatically every few minutes and labels each figure with the time its source last updated.

How big was the full 2026 Nebraska wildfire outbreak?

Fires burning from March 12 through early July 2026, including the Morrill, Cottonwood, South Fork, Ashby, Road 203 and Anderson Bridge fires, burned roughly 930,000 acres statewide. It prompted a federal major disaster declaration covering Arthur, Garden, Grant, Lincoln and Morrill counties.

Does lightning or human activity start more Nebraska wildfires?

Both play a role, but Nebraska's largest recent fires trace to non-lightning causes: the Morrill Fire is suspected to have started from electrical equipment, and the Road 702 Fire ignited in Kansas before spreading north. Dry lightning also triggers significant Nebraska fires in a typical year.
